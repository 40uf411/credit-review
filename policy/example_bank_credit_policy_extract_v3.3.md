# Example Bank — Residential Credit Policy (Extract)

**Document:** KRD-POL-2026/v3.3 (extract for renovation and acquisition credits, natural persons)
**Status:** Draft for approval · Owner: Credit Risk Committee
**Changes from v3.2:** article 5.3 adds point e (maximum term of renovation credits); article 5.4 raises the minimum own contribution from 2% to 10% of the total investment.
**Note:** This is a sanitized extract of the policy chapters relevant to file assessment. The full policy runs to 140 pages.

---

## 1. Scope

This policy applies to mortgage and renovation credits granted to natural persons for residential property located in Belgium. Files that do not meet one or more acceptance criteria are declined or escalated to the Credit Committee with a documented motivation.

## 2. Borrower and identity

2.1. Every applicant must be identified with a valid Belgian eID or passport. The national register number (rijksregisternummer) stated on the identity document must correspond with the number used on all other documents in the file.

2.2. The applicant must be at least 18 years old at application. The credit must be fully repaid before the applicant's **76th birthday**. For files with multiple applicants, the youngest applicant's age is used.

2.3. The identity document may not be expired at the date of the credit decision.

## 3. Income and affordability

3.1. Professional income of salaried applicants must be evidenced by **payslips for the three most recent months** and must be consistent with the income declared in the application. A declared professional income that deviates **more than 5%** from the average evidenced net income requires written clarification and is treated as an exception.

3.2. Income of self-employed applicants must be evidenced by the two most recent tax assessments (aanslagbiljetten). Where a personal tax assessment is available for any applicant, the declared income situation should be plausible in light of it.

3.3. Non-professional income (child benefit / Groeipakket, alimony received, rental income) may be counted **only where it is durable for the term of the credit and consistent with the household composition** stated in the file. Child benefit is counted at most until the youngest entitled child turns 18. The analyst documents the reasoning.

3.4. The **debt-service-to-income ratio (DSTI)** — all monthly credit obligations of the household, including the requested credit, divided by total counted net monthly income — may not exceed **45%**. Between 40% and 45% the file requires a motivated exception by a senior analyst.

3.5. The monthly repayment of the requested credit is computed on an annuity basis at the offered rate; where no rate is fixed yet, use the bank's reference rate of **4.0%** per annum.

3.6. After payment of all monthly credit obligations, the household must retain a **minimum free income of €1,200** per month, increased by €250 per dependent person.

## 4. Existing credit and credit history

4.1. A **CKP/BECRIS extract** must be present for every applicant and may not be older than one month at the credit decision.

4.2. Files showing a **registered payment arrear (wanbetaling)** that is not regularized are declined. Regularized arrears older than one year may be accepted with a motivated exception.

4.3. Every credit registered in the CKP/BECRIS extract must also appear in the liabilities declared in the application. Undeclared credits discovered in the extract are treated as a completeness exception and require clarification from the applicant.

## 5. Property, purpose and own contribution

5.1. The financed property must be located in Belgium and be identifiable through the purchase deed or purchase agreement (address, cadastral data). Property data stated across documents (address, year of construction, surface) must be consistent; unexplained discrepancies must be clarified before the decision.

5.2. For **acquisition** credits the loan-to-value ratio may not exceed **90%** of the purchase price (excluding costs). First-time buyers may go to 100% by exception.

5.3. For **renovation** credits:
   a. the applicant must be the owner or usufructuary of the property;
   b. a valid **EPC certificate** must be present for properties built before 2000, and may not be expired at the date of the application;
   c. where the EPC score exceeds **400 kWh/m²·year**, the planned works must include at least one energy-improving measure from the bank's eligible-measures list (insulation, heating installation, glazing, ventilation, solar);
   d. the requested amount must be plausible in relation to the described works;
   e. the credit term may not exceed **15 years**.

5.4. The applicant contributes **own funds of at least €1,000** or **10%** of the total investment, whichever is higher.

## 6. Documentation

A file is complete when it contains, at minimum:

| # | Document | Applies to |
|---|----------|-----------|
| D1 | Application form, signed | all files |
| D2 | Identity document (eID front and back) | every applicant |
| D3 | Payslips, 3 most recent months | salaried applicants |
| D4 | Personal tax assessment, most recent | every applicant |
| D5 | CKP/BECRIS extract, max 1 month old | every applicant |
| D6 | Purchase agreement or deed of the property | acquisition and renovation files |
| D7 | EPC certificate | renovation files, property built before 2000 |
| D8 | Bank statements of the salary account, 3 most recent months | salaried applicants |
| D9 | Certificate of conformity of the electrical installation | where required by regional regulation |

Missing documents are reported as completeness findings; the file may be pre-assessed but no offer may be issued while D1–D6 are missing.

## 7. Analyst judgment

7.1. Beyond the criteria above, the analyst forms an overall opinion on the **stability of the applicant's financial situation**: continuity of employment, the trend of income over the evidenced months, the plausibility of the declared household situation, and any signals of financial stress (salary advances, seizures on the payslip, structurally increasing credit usage).

7.2. Inconsistencies **between documents** — names, national register numbers, addresses, amounts, dates — must be flagged and either explained or escalated. Fraud signals are escalated immediately to the fraud desk and are never resolved by the analyst alone.

7.3. The analyst's conclusion is one of: **accept / accept with conditions / refer to Credit Committee / decline**, always with a written motivation that references the specific policy articles applied.
